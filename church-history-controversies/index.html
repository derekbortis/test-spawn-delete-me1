The controversy

What is the Filioque?

The Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed of 381 confessed that the Holy Spirit “proceeds from the Father.” Over the following centuries the Latin West added one word — Filioque, “and the Son” — so that the Spirit now proceeded “from the Father and the Son.”

To the West this clarified the Spirit's eternal relation to Christ. To the East it was a unilateral tampering with an ecumenical Creed and a theological error that confused the Persons of the Trinity. It became the central doctrinal fault line of the Great Schism of 1054 — and it divides the Catholic and Orthodox churches to this day.

A note on method

Reading the fully developed doctrine back into the earliest Fathers is anachronistic — most wrote before the question was sharply posed. The honest map is not “for vs. against” but a spectrum: explicit double procession in the Latin West, the contested middle ground of “through the Son,” and the Eastern insistence on the Father as sole cause.
